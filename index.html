Read this before anything else

Three things that decide whether this roadmap works for you or becomes another list of bookmarks.

The honest verdict on paid content

Most of the best AI education on earth is free — and that is not a consolation prize. Karpathy, Stanford CS336, Hugging Face, LangChain Academy and Anthropic Academy are genuinely the best resources in existence for their topics, and they cost nothing.

Spend money on exactly four categories:

  1. Books you will return to for years. ~$45–65 each, one-time. Best value in the entire list.
  2. One structured course when you need momentum, not knowledge — usually early, when self-direction is hardest.
  3. Compute. Colab Pro or ~$50 of API credits will teach you more than a $200 course. The single most underrated line item.
  4. Nothing else.

The three habits that separate you from people who "did a course"

  1. Build the thing before you read about the thing. Struggle first, then read. The reading lands ten times harder against a problem you have already hit.
  2. Write it down publicly. A repo, a blog, notes in your team wiki. If you cannot explain it in writing, you do not know it — and this is exactly what makes your team notice.
  3. Read one paper a week from Phase 5 onward. Not to understand every equation — to build tolerance for primary sources. Most engineers never do this. It compounds absurdly.

The failure mode to avoid

Completing forty courses and being unable to build anything unassisted. The defence is the Build box in every module. If you skip the builds, this roadmap is worthless. If you do only the builds and half the reading, you will be fine.

On unpredictable hours: never break the chain on Phase 4.1 (Karpathy) or Phase 8.3 (LangGraph) — those two need continuity. In zero-hour weeks, read; reading survives interruption, coding does not. And it is entirely reasonable to pull Phase 8.3 forward out of order, because your job needs it now.

    What "knowing everything" actually looks like

    Not memorising every paper. Nobody knows everything in this field — it moves too fast. The people who look like they do are the ones with strong foundations who close gaps quickly and publicly.

    • 01You can implement the core of anything you use, from scratch, at least once.
    • 02You can say why a design choice was made, and what the alternative was.
    • 03You know where each thing fails, and you say so before someone else finds out.
    • 04You measure. You show numbers. You do not say "it seems better."
    • 05You can disagree with a popular opinion and defend it — the way you would hold both the Anthropic and Cognition views on multi-agent systems at once.
    • 06You say "I don't know, let me find out" quickly — and then you actually find out.

    That last one is the whole thing. Foundations are what this roadmap buys you. The closing-gaps-quickly part is a habit, and you start it today.
